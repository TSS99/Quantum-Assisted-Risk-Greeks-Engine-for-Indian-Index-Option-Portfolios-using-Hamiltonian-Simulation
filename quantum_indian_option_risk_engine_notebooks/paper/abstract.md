# Abstract

We present a reproducible notebook-based research workflow for quantum-assisted portfolio risk and Greeks reconstruction for Indian index option portfolios using non-Hermitian Hamiltonian simulation of Black-Scholes dynamics. The study begins with the Black-Scholes partial differential equation, applies the log-price transformation and time reversal, and represents the resulting dynamics through a non-Hermitian Black-Scholes Hamiltonian. Momentum-space diagonalization is implemented with QFT-style transforms, while the anti-Hermitian component is simulated through a one-ancilla unitary dilation and ancilla post-selection. The reconstructed option price curves are used to estimate price errors and Greeks, including Delta, Gamma, Vega, Theta, and Rho.

Unlike single-option demonstrations, the workflow emphasizes industry-facing risk outputs. It supports synthetic Indian market data, user-provided NSE-style option-chain CSV files, and manual YAML portfolio configuration. Case studies cover NIFTY 50 and Bank NIFTY option portfolios, stress P&L, volatility sensitivity, Monte Carlo P&L simulation, VaR, Expected Shortfall, finite-difference PDE benchmarking, boundary duplication, post-selection probability, volatility-surface interpolation, and resource scaling through 40 qubits. Large statevector experiments are guarded by explicit memory estimates and safe fallbacks.

The project does not claim quantum advantage, classical outperformance, or production readiness. Instead, it studies whether a quantum Hamiltonian simulation workflow can reproduce option prices, Greeks, stress P&L, and tail-risk metrics with controlled numerical error when benchmarked against classical Black-Scholes, finite-difference, and Monte Carlo baselines. The result is a transparent research scaffold for evaluating feasibility, accuracy, scaling limits, and practical risk-engine relevance.
