# Paper Outline

## Introduction
Motivation for quantum-assisted risk workflows and why portfolio Greeks matter more than isolated pricing examples.

## Related Work
Quantum finance, Hamiltonian simulation, Black-Scholes quantum mappings, amplitude methods, and classical risk-engine baselines.

## Financial Model
European Indian index option assumptions, Black-Scholes baseline, Greeks, finite-difference PDE, stress P&L, VaR, and Expected Shortfall.

## Quantum Hamiltonian Formulation
Log-price transform, time reversal, momentum operator, non-Hermitian Black-Scholes Hamiltonian, QFT diagonalization, unitary dilation, and post-selection.

## Portfolio Risk Engine
NSE-style CSV ingestion, synthetic Indian option-chain generation, manual portfolio config, valuation, Greeks aggregation, stress testing, and tail-risk metrics.

## Experiments
Classical validation, finite-difference benchmark, quantum single-option pricing, quantum Greeks, portfolio risk, boundary duplication, post-selection probability, scaling, and volatility surface.

## Results
Accuracy tables, error curves, Greeks comparison, stress P&L heatmaps, VaR/ES plots, and resource estimates.

## Limitations
No quantum advantage claim, state preparation costs, memory scaling, hardware depth and noise, Black-Scholes simplifications, and calibration needs.

## Conclusion
Summary of feasibility findings and research directions.
